forecasting · arc forecaster v1

Predicting where a style goes next.

This map measures how players play, not how good they are — so a forecast predicts a style vector, not a stat line.

Arc Forecaster v1 predicts a player's next-season embedding: given the trail so far, where in the 64-d space does the style land next year? It does not predict points, wins, or awards.

Plate 02 · the honest note about the MAE

The MAE is in embedding space, not points. A macro-MAE of 0.4382 is the average distance between the predicted 64-d style vector and the actual 64-d style vector on held-out seasons. It is not "off by 0.44 points per game." It answers "how close did we get to the shape of next season's game?" — a harder, stranger question than predicting a stat line, and the number only means something next to the baselines it beat.

Why forecast style instead of stats? Because the embedding is the shared language of the whole system — twins, trails, and the map all speak it. A forecaster that predicts the vector plugs into everything; a forecaster that predicts points plugs into nothing.

Data flag, kept in the open. During development, the mpg (minutes per game) field was found uncorrelated with real minutes — it could not be trusted, so it was not used. Real 2025-26 minutes were used instead. A model is only as honest as the fields it refuses.

Plate 03 · how to read a forecast

  • A forecast is a direction, not a destiny. It says "players on this kind of trail usually drift here" — injuries, trades, and breakouts live outside the model's sight.
  • Young players are harder. Short trails give the forecaster less to work with; the honest read is wider uncertainty on second- and third-year players.
  • It inherits the stat scope. No lineup data, no matchup context, defense via steals/blocks only — the forecast cannot see a role change coming, only the statistical shadow after it lands.
