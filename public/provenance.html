Plate 03 · what we don't know

Where the numbers come from is only half the ledger. The other half is how much to trust them — an explicit, dated list (2026-10-18):

  • Similarity scores are uncalibrated point estimates. A twin at 0.886 and a near-miss at 0.884 may not be meaningfully different — there are no confidence intervals, so treat close scores as ties, not rankings.
  • Era comparisons span data-quality regimes. Pre-2000s tracking data is thinner, so older seasons' vectors rest on less detail. Direction of bias: older players' styles may look simpler — and therefore more "modern-comparable" — than they were.
  • PLUS_MINUS is a noisy on/off estimate, not a skill rating. It measures what happened while a player was on the floor, tangled with teammates, opponents, and luck. Direction of bias: noisy toward zero in small samples; never read it as value.
  • The 64-d space is a style map, and style similarity is not value similarity. A fun comp is not a claim about who was better. Direction of bias: the map flatters stylistic novelty — unusual games stand out — which is interesting, not better.

source: overhaul plan §6a (Hullman's "what we don't know" amendment, 2026-10-18). This section is the point of the page.
