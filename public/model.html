model card · the champion

One model serves the whole map.

The hoops embedding model is a multi-tower neural network (MTNN), version 5. It reads a player-season's box-score stats and outputs 64 numbers — a position in style-space. One champion serves every surface; nothing else is allowed to answer.

Plate 02 · what the network takes in

Each player-season enters the model as 14 numbers: per-100-possession box-score stats (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, shooting splits, and friends), each z-scored within its own season so a 1962 season and a 2024 season speak the same language. That is the entire input. No lineup data, no matchup context, no tracking data — defense is represented only through steals and blocks.

source: input schema per overhaul plan §6b (stat-scope statement); full scope on /embeddings.

Plate 03 · what the 64 numbers mean

Each tower of the network reads one family of stats; the towers' outputs are concatenated and fused down to a 64-number vector. Those 64 numbers are not 64 named things — they are a position. Two player-seasons whose vectors point nearly the same direction played the game in a similar style. Closeness is measured with cosine similarity: the angle between two arrows, not the length of either.

Think of it like a fingerprint made of playing style. The model never sees a player's name, team, or era — only the 14 stats. If two far-apart eras land near each other, that is the stats talking, not nostalgia.

source: architecture family and fusion per model name mtnn_v5_concat_b2_h160_t32_d64_mlp128_fus256; similarity metric per era-twins method (true argmax in served 64-d), plan §1.4.

Plate 04 · why 64 dimensions

Sixty-four is the champion's width — a hyperparameter, not a law of nature. It is wide enough to hold the variation across 14 stats and eight decades, and narrow enough to serve fast. Any challenger at any width may take the crown, but only by beating v5 on held-out evaluation. The dimension count is an engineering choice that earned its keep; the gates that keep it honest are on /methodology.

Honesty note. A bigger number is not a better model. Past drafts of this site once printed a "v6 / 128-d" claim that was never the served model — see the drift changelog on /provenance. The served model is v5, 64-d. Everything on this site is written against that.

Plate 05 · the single-model rule

One champion serves every surface — the games, the atlas, the twin lookups, the trails. There is no A/B shadow model answering half the traffic, no "experimental" embedding leaking into production. When a challenger wins (see /methodology), it replaces the champion wholesale, and the asset is re-served. This is the anti-drift guarantee: the map you see and the map we evaluate are the same map.
