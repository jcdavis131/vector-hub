methodology · frameworks & gates

How a model earns the crown.

The framework is a multi-tower neural network. The discipline around it matters more: one champion serves, challengers train offline, and no promotion without a held-out win.

Plate 01 · the framework

MTNN — multi-tower neural network. Each "tower" is a small network that reads one family of the 14 input stats (scoring, rebounding, playmaking, defense, efficiency…). The towers' outputs are concatenated and fused through an MLP down to the 64-d embedding. The design bet: letting each stat family get its own representation before fusion preserves structure that a single flat layer would smear.

v5 is the fifth major version of this design. The version number counts promoted champions — v5 beat v4 on held-out evaluation, v4 beat v3, and so on. There is no v6 in production; past copy that said otherwise was wrong (see /provenance).

sources: MTNN family and concat-fusion per architecture name mtnn_v5_concat_b2_h160_t32_d64_mlp128_fus256; version discipline per project records.

Plate 02 · champion vs challenger

  1. One champion serves. MTNN v5 answers every query (single-model policy). It is never retrained in place.
  2. Challengers train offline. New architectures, widths, and features are trained as challengers against the same corpus.
  3. Challengers are evaluated on held-out data the champion never saw — including twin-stability identity checks (do the known twin pairs survive?) and representation-similarity checks (CKA) against the champion.
  4. Promotion is wholesale. A challenger that clears every gate replaces the champion entirely; the served asset is re-cut and the version number advances. A challenger that fails stays offline.

source: champion-vs-challenger protocol and identity checks per overhaul plan §1.8 and project records (twin-stability + CKA checks from the standing retrain protocol).

Plate 03 · the held-out promotion gates

A challenger must clear all of these. One miss, no crown:

  • Held-out win. The challenger must beat the champion on data neither model saw in training. No promotion without it — this is the load-bearing gate.
  • Calibration gate: ECE < 0.05. Where the model reports confidence, its expected calibration error must stay under 0.05 — predicted probabilities must mean what they say.
  • Twin-stability. The established twin pairs must substantially survive the swap; a "better" model that scrambles every known comp is a different map, not a better one.
  • No synthetic leakage. Training and evaluation data are real recorded games only. Synthetic or imputed rows anywhere in the eval path fail the run.

Why the gates are strict. The map is downstream of everything — games, twins, trails, forecasts. A quiet regression in the embedding would corrupt all of them at once, and style-space has no single "accuracy" number to catch it. The gates are the immune system.

source: gates per overhaul plan §1.8 (ECE < 0.05, no promotion without a held-out win) and project records.
