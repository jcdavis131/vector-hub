era twins · the method

Who played most like you — from another era?

This map measures how players play, not how good they are — so a "twin" is a style match, not a greatness ranking.

A player's era twin is the true argmax other-decade career in the served 64-d space: among every player-season whose peak sits in a different decade, the one whose vector points closest to yours. No hand-picking, no narrative — just the nearest neighbor with the era constraint.

Plate 01 · the computation

  1. Take every player-season's 64-d vector from the served model (MTNN v5).
  2. For a given player, consider only player-seasons from other decades — the twin must come from another era by construction.
  3. Rank those candidates by cosine similarity to the player's vector.
  4. The twin is the argmax: the single highest-similarity candidate. The true argmax — the search runs over all 1,313 players, not a shortlist.

On 2026-10-12 the twin list was regenerated on v5 (PR #90): every one of the 1,313 players got the true argmax other-decade career in the served 64-d space, and 79% of players got a new twin. The old list had been computed against a stale model; the regeneration replaced opinion with measurement.

Plate 02 · worked examples, real numbers

Curry 2015-16 ↔ SGA 2024-25 — mutual. Stephen Curry's 2015-16 season (unanimous MVP, 402 threes) and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's 2024-25 season are each other's era twin: Curry's nearest other-decade neighbor is SGA 2024-25, and SGA's nearest other-decade neighbor is Curry 2015-16. Two on-ball engines, a decade apart, converged on the same corner of style-space.

LeBron 2008-09 → Durant 2013-14 @ 0.886. LeBron James's 2008-09 season — first MVP, two-way freight train — has its nearest other-decade neighbor in Kevin Durant's 2013-14 MVP season, at cosine similarity 0.886 in the served 64-d. The arrow points one way here: LeBron's twin is Durant; the method makes no promise the feeling is mutual.

Honesty note. These are style matches, not greatness claims — how they played, not how good they were. And 0.886 is an uncalibrated point estimate: there is no confidence interval, and a near-tie at 0.884 would be a coin flip, not a verdict. See /provenance.

sources: examples per overhaul plan §1.4 (Curry 2015-16 ↔ SGA 2024-25 mutual; LeBron 2008-09 → Durant 2013-14 @ 0.886).

Plate 03 · the twin explainer chips

When a twin is revealed, a chip explains why in plain words. The method: for the two 64-d vectors, find the dimensions where they differ least — the smallest |a_d − b_d| — and translate those dimensions into plain-words labels, e.g. "both outscore opponents on court" (the on/off estimate dimension) rather than jargon.

The chips deliberately describe shared dimensions — what the two seasons have in common — because the twin claim is "these two played alike," and the evidence for it is the overlap. Wording is kept factual: "both outscore opponents on court," never "both tilt the floor."

source: chip method per overhaul plan §1.4 (shared dims = smallest |a_d − b_d|, plain-words labels); 2026-10-18 wording fixes per project records.
